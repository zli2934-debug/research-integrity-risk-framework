# Formal Logic Rules

## Rule 1: Retraction is not identical to fraud

A retraction indicates that an article should not be relied upon as part of the scholarly record. It does not automatically prove intentional misconduct.

## Rule 2: Public concern is not a final verdict

PubPeer comments, self-media findings, and online comparisons are signals. They require verification against original data and official records.

## Rule 3: Anomaly requires alternative-explanation testing

Before classifying an anomaly as manipulation risk, evaluate benign explanations such as formatting error, figure assembly mistake, PDF compression, SD/SEM confusion, statistical-method ambiguity, instrument precision, rounding, or publisher error.

## Rule 4: Confirmed misconduct requires official wording

Use C5 only when an official notice or investigation explicitly states misconduct, fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, image manipulation, fake peer review, paper-mill involvement, or equivalent terms.

## Rule 5: Repeated misconduct pattern requires repeated formal evidence

Do not classify an author as showing a repeated misconduct pattern unless identity is confirmed and multiple formal records show similar problems.

## Rule 6: Paper-level risk is not person-level responsibility

A paper can show strong data-integrity risk without proving that every author intentionally engaged in misconduct. Responsibility requires contribution records or formal attribution.

## Rule 7: TRS is not a verdict

The weighted score supports preliminary classification. C5 and C6 require hard logic conditions beyond TRS.


## v0.2.1-alpha audit-fix rules

- C6 requires clear responsibility, confirmed author identity, repeated similar pattern, multiple formal records, and official misconduct-type wording.
- C7 cannot automatically override high TRS or high core-module abnormalities.
- TRS >= 4 remains a TRS-only high-risk category (`C3+`) unless formal records support C4-C7.
- This framework is a scoring framework, not a database analyzer or adjudication system.
